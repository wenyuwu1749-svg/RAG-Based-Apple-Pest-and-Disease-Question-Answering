# New Zealand apple pest and disease supplement: collection and admission report

Date: 2026-10-13. The scope follows formal research scope v1.0, including postharvest storage and transport and excluding regulations and species status.

This round collected material from the public New Zealand Plant Protection Society website and journal and from HortInfo technical pages. Added **30 sources: 9 HTML pages, 21 PDFs, 133 PDF pages and 125 QA chunks (44 web + 81 PDF)**.

Database at this point: 65 PDFs with 1,123 pages and 199 web pages, 4,023 retrievable chunks in total. The existing 3,898 chunks, the 34 + 10 PDF register entries and the existing page texts were compared item by item with the backup taken before admission and were identical.

## Content not admitted or restricted

- Semi-commercial hot-water treatment paper (NZ16/S276): no publicly downloadable full text was obtained; the web abstract is not presented as a complete management protocol.
- CEDA, September 2025, *Apples in Manawatū: A Grower Guide* (NZ26/S286): 19 pages are images and no text can be extracted; the downloaded file and visual check record are kept pending OCR, and no text is invented.
- Some journal PDFs contain several abstracts on one page: from S278 only the apple mealybug abstract is taken, and from S289 only the apple Elsinoe abstract; carrot and other insect content is excluded. Neither is treated as a complete field programme.
- S271 (2008 IFP): only the reliably extracted abstract is admitted; the remaining text with broken encoding is not retrieved. S290 washing/storage trial: sentences with corrupted numbers are excluded. The complete original text remains in the PDF.
- In the NZPPS 2024/2025 updated pages, old product registration tables, programmes for other crops and the 2004 resistance survey are not labelled as current advice; only technical passages applicable to apple are kept.
- From the new web pages, only two rows of an apple-specific resistance management table are admitted, keeping the full header, columns and regional exceptions; token-efficient RAG still treats each table unit as a whole.

## Retrieval and data verification

- SQLite integrity_check = ok; source-file SHA-256, PDF page and text ranges, and original and cleaned HTML SHA-256 are stored together; the backup taken before the update can be restored.
- Each of the 30 new sources was checked with lexical retrieval (used at that stage) and all were hit; for 7 of 8 ordinary English questions a new source was in the top 10. A broad woolly aphid question did not retrieve the new NZ evidence; it did after adding the specific study conditions (Nelson, Aphelinus). Both retrieval records are kept and the first result is not hidden.
- The above is only an offline retrieval check, not an evaluation of answer accuracy. No LLM or embedding model was called and no vector index was built.
- Retrieval at this point had no NZ region filtering or priority, so general overseas content may rank higher. Region filtering and topic labels had to be verified before the formal experiment; configuring an embedding model does not by itself ensure regional applicability.
- The Phytophthora label for fruit rot was corrected in the new data and is not treated as root/crown rot. The old general keyword labeller still has this ambiguity, so labels need re-checking after all PDFs are re-extracted; the QA program was not changed in this round.
